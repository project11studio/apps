Stopline Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 12, 2026.

Stopline is built by Project11, an independent developer in Ontario, Canada ("we", "us"). This policy explains what Stopline does with information. The short version: blocking happens entirely on your phone. What Stopline sees on your screen never leaves it.

1. What Stopline reads on your phone, and why

Stopline uses Android's Accessibility Service to recognise which screen is open in supported apps (Instagram, YouTube, Facebook, Snapchat and WeChat). It looks at the layout of the screen, such as the names of buttons, tabs and lists. That's how it tells a Reels player from a direct message, or the Facebook home feed from a group. When it recognises a feed you chose to block, it presses Back, switches to a safe tab (such as Snapchat's Chat), or draws a cover over the feed.

  • This happens only on your device, only while the service is on, and only for those apps.
  • Stopline does not record, store or transmit what is on your screen, including posts, videos, messages, contacts, account names or anything you type.
  • It never types, taps or acts inside other apps beyond pressing Back, drawing a cover, or scrolling a covered feed back to the top when you pull down on the cover. It never stops you from turning it off or uninstalling it.

If you turn on Fix detection, Stopline can save a "capture" of a screen's structure (element types, ids and positions) to help fix a block that isn't working. Captures stay on your phone. You can view, share or delete them in the app, and nothing is sent unless you choose to share one yourself.

2. Information stored on your phone

Your settings, which blocks are on, daily counts (feeds dodged, pauses, focus sessions, emergency unlocks, and the reasons you picked for switching a block off), your focus-session plan and your emergency reasons. If you use Friend lock, Stopline stores only a salted hash of the code, never the code itself. Uninstalling Stopline deletes all of it.

Screen-time check, focus tasks and XP. The screen-time numbers you type in, your focus tasks and your XP and level are stored only on your phone. They are not included in shared counts.

Habits and plans. Your "why are you here" answers, your instead-of list and if-then plans, your bedtime hours, your nightly 1-to-5 ratings, today's three, badges, streak freezes and the photo you pick for "your why" are stored only on your phone. They are not included in shared counts.

Pause check-ins, urge surfing and experiments. If you rate your mood or guess how long you'll stay before a pause, how long the pause really lasted, your 1-to-5 urge ratings, your one-week experiments and their before-and-after results are stored only on your phone. They are not included in shared counts.

Settings backup (optional). "Back up settings" writes a file to the place you pick in Android's file picker (for example your Downloads folder or a cloud drive you choose). It contains your block switches, modes, your plan's words (instead-of list, if-then plans, values, bedtime) and focus templates. It never contains your counts, XP, leaderboard keys, random ID or Friend lock code. Stopline does not upload it anywhere itself; where it goes is up to you.

Usage access (optional). If you grant Android's Usage access, Stopline reads your total phone use for the last 7 days on the phone and keeps only the daily average. It does not store or send which apps you used.

Focus sounds. Built-in sounds are generated on the phone. If you pick your own audio file, Stopline only keeps permission to play it.

Notifications. If you allow them, Stopline sends at most one quiet reminder per period you choose (or never) to turn Fix detection back on. Reminders are created on your phone; nothing is sent to us.

3. Information that leaves your phone

Stopline only goes online in three ways:

WhatWhenWhat is sent
Detection updatesEvery few hours, and when you open StoplineA request for the latest block rules. Nothing about you or your phone is included beyond what any internet request carries (such as your IP address, which our host may log briefly).
Requests and suggestionsOnly when you tap SendThe text you wrote, the app you picked (if any), and the Stopline version.
Daily counts (optional, off by default)Once a day, only if you turn on "Help improve Stopline"For each day: feeds dodged, pauses and paused minutes, focus sessions, emergency unlocks, how many times a block was switched off and which preset reasons were chosen, whether Friend lock and Buzz are on, which blocks are on, the Stopline version, and a random ID created on your phone.

Leaderboard (optional, off by default). If you join, we store the nickname you choose, your city, country and age band if you give them, your friend code and who you've added as a friend. Other players on the same board see only your nickname and score. Scores are calculated on our server from the daily counts above. Joining requires daily counts to be on. "Leave the leaderboard" deletes your entry and friends list immediately. Players who choose the 13–17 age band only appear on their friends' boards. If you turn on "Study with a friend", your friends also see whether you are focusing, finished or left early, and when your session ends; never your tasks. The daily counts include whether a streak freeze covered that day. Invite links contain only your friend code.

The random ID isn't linked to your name, email, phone number, Google account, advertising ID or device ID. It only lets us tell one phone's days apart from another's, for example to see whether people who use Friend lock dodge more over time.

We do not collect your location, contacts, messages, photos, browsing, the accounts or videos you see, or any advertising identifier. We do not sell or rent information, use it for advertising, or share it with data brokers.

4. How we use it

  • To fix and improve blocking.
  • To decide which apps and features to build next.
  • To understand which methods (pauses, friend lock, focus sessions, buzz) help people scroll less.

Aggregated, anonymous findings may be published, for example in a research paper or blog post. They are never published in a form that identifies a person. If we ever run a formal research study, it will be separate and opt-in, with its own consent form and ethics approval.

5. Where it's stored and for how long

Requests and daily counts are stored with our database provider, Supabase, on servers in Canada (ca-central-1), encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Daily counts are kept for up to 3 years and requests for up to 2 years, then deleted or reduced to totals that can't be traced back to anyone.

6. Your choices and rights

  • Turn off sharing at any time in Stopline → Settings → Help improve Stopline. Nothing more is sent after that.
  • Delete what was sent: email us and we'll delete the daily counts linked to your random ID. Include roughly when you turned sharing on and your phone model so we can find the right ID.
  • Depending on where you live (including under Canada's PIPEDA, Quebec's Law 25 for Quebec residents, the EU/UK GDPR and California's CCPA), you may have rights to access, correct, delete or move your information, or to complain to a regulator. Email us to use them; we'll answer within 30 days.

7. Children

Stopline is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has sent us a request, email us and we'll delete it.

8. Changes

If we change what Stopline collects, we'll update this page and the date at the top. If the change is significant, we'll also show it in the app before it takes effect. We will never start sending more without asking.
